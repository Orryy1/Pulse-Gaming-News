# Pulse Gaming Visual Identity

## Style Prompt

Pulse Gaming is a fast, evidence-led gaming newsroom with the confidence of a premium broadcast package and the immediacy of a top creator. The visual signature is a warm amber signal cutting through near-black editorial structure, supported by cool verification accents, compact proof language and real game motion. Pulse must remain recognisable when the logo is small, the video is muted or the underlying game footage changes completely.

## Brand Memory Cues

- Persistent `PULSE // GAMING` signature in the lower-right safe zone.
- A dual-rail pulse: amber is the identity signal, cyan is reserved for verification and proof.
- Story-aware segment labels such as `BREAKING PULSE`, `TRAILER TRUTH`, `PATCH PULSE`, `PLATFORM PULSE` and `WISHLIST CHECK`.
- Proof language uses `PULSE PROOF` and `PLAYER IMPACT`.
- The final lockup reads `PULSE GAMING` and `NEVER MISS A BEAT`.
- Source branding remains subordinate to Pulse Gaming.

## Colours

- Pulse amber, primary identity: `#FF6B1A`
- Signal ink, background: `#0D0D0F`
- Warm paper, primary text: `#F0F0F0`
- Signal ice, verified text: `#BEEBFF`
- Verification cyan, proof only: `#38BDF8`

Amber must be the dominant memory cue. Cyan may confirm or annotate evidence but must not turn the design into a generic cyan-on-dark technology template.

## Typography

- Display and headlines: Bahnschrift, heavy or condensed where available.
- Metadata, source labels and evidence: Consolas.
- Captions: heavy condensed display styling with amber emphasis, large mobile-safe sizing and natural phrase grouping.
- Numbers in evidence cards use tabular figures.
- Source labels never compete with the headline or game subject.

## Motion

- The spoken hook starts immediately. Branding lands with it and never delays it.
- Opening identity resolves within 1.6 seconds.
- Primary transition language: fast editorial push or clean source-lock wipe.
- Accent transition: brief signal glitch or amber flash at a genuine narrative turn.
- Use one ambient movement per scene. Do not stack drifting overlays, zoom and scan motion together.
- Proof cards build, breathe and resolve. A source card is a beat, not a scene.
- The CTA lockup appears only in the final 2.8 seconds.

## Composition

- Real game motion is the visual backbone.
- Keep at least two focal points and three depth layers in designed frames.
- Use edge-anchored editorial zones rather than centred floating cards.
- Preserve Instagram and YouTube interface safe zones.
- Captions remain visually dominant over metadata but never cover the principal face, character or gameplay action.
- Repeated clips, repeated HyperFrames card kinds and long static source cards are production blockers.

## What NOT to Do

- Do not lead with a logo animation before the hook.
- Do not let Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo, Steam or an outlet become the strongest brand in the frame.
- Do not use long full-screen source cards, generic glass dashboards or article-dominated decks.
- Do not use gradient orbs, bokeh blobs, purple-blue technology gradients or decorative card grids.
- Do not use tiny metadata, overlapping top-left stacks or captions formatted like `G T A SIX` or `twenty twenty six`.
- Do not repeat the same transition, clip family, SFX or caption animation throughout one video.
- Do not imitate a competitor's template, branding or assets.
