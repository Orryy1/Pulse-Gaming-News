# Comment mining, 26 October 2026

I read 12,679 top comments on 142 niche videos: 70 narrated hit Shorts, 22 narrated low-band Shorts and the 50 most-commented long videos. The 6 most-liked comments on every video (852) were classified by hand; topic and complaint counts come from keywords and are approximate. Raw data is in the session scratchpad at `research2/comments/`.

## What the top comments do (hit Shorts)

| Comment type                    | Share of comments | Share of likes |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------- |
| A joke on a line from the video | 34%               | 45%            |
| Argues a take                   | 16%               | 16%            |
| Answers the video's question    | 13%               | 13%            |
| Adds a fact                     | 11%               | 13%            |
| Corrects the video              | 8%                | 2.5%           |
| Nostalgia                       | 7%                | 4%             |

Hit and low Shorts drew the same kinds of comments. What made the difference was the topic: a villain or a money pain, lore with a human twist, an underdog, a design secret.

## Closing questions

| Closing type                                                       | Top comments that answered |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Specific (named fork, three options, "name one", your own numbers) | 73%                        |
| Generic ("thoughts?")                                              | 46%                        |
| "Will you play it?"                                                | 33%                        |
| Rhetorical or punchline                                            | 12%                        |

48 of the 70 hit Shorts asked nothing. Title questions get answered before closing lines do.

## What viewers are arguing about now

- Older gaming was better.
- Physical against digital ownership.
- Silksong.
- GTA 6.
- $70-$100 prices and waiting for sales.
- Distrust of review scores.
- Sony's dynamic pricing and disc-less plans: the most likes per comment of any topic.
- Retro prices, scalpers and trade-ins.

Sony is the villain, CD Projekt the redeemed hero and Game Freak the punchline.

## What viewers punish

- Corrections land on "what happens if", lore and "every/ultimate/complete" lists.
- Drama titles about trivial details ("BIG problem") get mocked.
- "No one talks about this" draws "everyone noticed" as the top comment.
- Leak Shorts draw "grain of salt" replies.
- AI hostility is common ("slop"). The most-liked meta comment in the Shorts praised a Short for having no AI voice, no music bed and no flashy captions, and for getting straight to the point.

## What changed in the factory

- **Desk question:** voices the sceptic's objection, and the take answers it head on.
- **Loop line:**
  - when it asks, it asks something answerable in five words (a named fork, three options, "name one", the viewer's own numbers), with money in dollars first;
  - never rhetorical, a punchline or "will you play it?".
- **Story editor:** told which arguments viewers are having now.
- **Titles:** no "BIG problem" over small details, no "no one is talking about", and no "every/ultimate/complete" unless the video truly covers it all.
- **search_gaps.md:** gains 12 viewer questions as why and ranking ideas. The pickers now see up to 60.
