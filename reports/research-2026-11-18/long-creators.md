# Long-form creators: what the best humans do that Pulse's long videos do not

Studied 18 Nov 2026 for Pulse Gaming. 25 long videos measured from vidIQ transcripts, metadata, comments and eight "watch" reports. No factory code or settings were changed. Builds on the 4 Nov inspiration forensics and the 25 Oct documentary study; nothing there is repeated.

**Labels:** M = Measured (computed from transcript, metadata or comments saved in `raw-long/`). S = Snippet (reported by vidIQ's watch tool or a description, not verified frame by frame). I = Inferred.

## Summary in ten lines

1. The best long videos are carried by **people with a point of view**, not by a narrator reading history. "The Long Road" has 15 named voices and its narrator says nothing for the first 3 min 22 s (M).
2. Every hit has **one finish line** set early and reached late: a rank goal, a three-day build, a title paradox, a scandal that nearly ends the studio. Underperformers plant none (M).
3. **The question lands inside 90 seconds** and is often half-answered at once. The same narrator's flop asked three abstract questions, then gave a definition (M).
4. **Ranking hits are arguments, not catalogues.** "I" appears 19 to 40 times per 1,000 words and roughly 40 to 48% of sentences carry a judgement. Disagreement is invited in the first 30 s (M, I).
5. **Commentary that holds states its verdict by 0:00 to 2:15** and is built as numbered claims, each tested in 3 to 8 minutes (M).
6. **Music follows the story.** The Long Road changes bed about 12 times in 50 minutes, each matched to the era on screen, and cuts to silence at every chapter card (S).
7. **Intros cut 1.5 to 2.5 times faster than bodies** (18 to 31 cuts a minute against 8 to 14) (S).
8. **Explainers build their own pictures:** 21 to 25% of runtime is custom 3D recreation or schematics (S).
9. **Pace varies; there is no magic speed.** Hits run from 118 to 214 words a minute. The flop ran flat and fast for an hour (M).
10. **Packaging sells one thing to find out**, in 0 to 4 thumbnail words that add to the title. A copied thumbnail template with no promise got 0.53x (M).

**An honest caveat.** In views, this is not the channel's biggest recent hit. The same creator's 26-minute "GTA 6's Driving Physics Explained" reached 666,435 views and 9.7x. The Long Road is the craft benchmark, and the timely explainer is the reach benchmark. Episode 2 kept 36% of Episode 1's views, which matches the earlier finding that numbered parts shed about half.

### What makes it great, in one paragraph

It is an oral history. The story of Rockstar is told by a man who built the games and by 14 people who grew up inside them, intercut with the news footage of the day, each year dressed in its own music and its own screen. The narrator is a host who sets the question and then gets out of the way. The episode ends at the studio's lowest moment, not at a neat conclusion.

### Sound map (S)

- **Twelve music beds in 50 minutes**, about 2.4 per 10 minutes. Each bed is the era: chiptune groove for 1987-1999, driving synth for GTA III, synthwave for Vice City, low drone and strings for the moral panic, West Coast groove for San Andreas, pulsing "investigation" synth for Hot Coffee.
- **Silence is punctuation.** No music for the first 28 seconds. Hard drop-outs at 0:27, 3:43, every year card and 49:27.
- **Real-world sound sells the archive:** CRT hum, muffled news microphones, hall acoustics, VHS buzz.
- **Game sound is a character:** tyres, sirens, pedestrian screams, cheat beeps, radio tuning.
- **Small tactile effects:** a clock flip on year cards, camera shutters on photographs, page turns on notebooks.
- The auto-captions carry 63 music tags in 45 blocks (M), so music is audible under most of the talk, ducked under voices.

### Community (M)

Of the likes on the top 100 comments, 46% sit on two comments celebrating the cast ("GTA Avengers"). The pinned creator comment is the sponsor line. Viewers praise who is in it and the effort, not the facts.

### What transfers to a faceless channel, and what does not

- **Transfers:** real archive voices in the first 30 seconds; a chorus of short quoted voices before the narrator; era-matched music; silence at chapter seams; the contrast cut; ending on a threat; object and CRT framing; launching a series in the week of the event.
- **Does not transfer directly:** 15 recorded interviews. The honest substitute is archive clips and on-screen quotes from named primary sources, and Martin's own recorded takes.

## 3. The craft patterns that separate them from automated long videos

1. **A chorus before the narrator.** The Long Road opens with archive and nine voices; narration starts at 3:22 (M). Driving Physics cuts to a comedy clip at 0:49 and an engineer at 3:29; it has 33 speaker changes in 27 minutes (M). Pulse has one voice from first second to last.
2. **One finish line.** NakeyJakey sets a rank goal at 5:25 and reaches it at 1:18:35. People Make Games sets "build the map in three days" at 0:48 and finishes at about 46:30. Raycevick pays its title paradox at 31:42. The Long Road ends on Hot Coffee. The flops (Open Worlds, 100 jam games) have no loop (M).
3. **Question inside 90 seconds, half-answered at once.** 0:00, 0:16, 0:45, 1:29 (answered at 1:38). The flop asked three abstract questions at 1:09 to 1:25, then defined its terms (M).
4. **Rankings are a person's verdicts.** "I" 19 to 40 per 1,000 words against 1.8 in the encyclopaedic Army Men history. DIBZ ends all 23 entries with a spoken verdict and a lock-in sound (S). The fan-vote version of the same ranking, with no host verdict, got 10K views against 1.9M (M).
5. **Rankings invite the fight early and collect it.** Invitations at 0:03, 0:23 and 0:50. 27% of DIBZ's comment likes go to placement arguments; its pinned comment asks for viewers' best and worst (311 likes, 69 replies) (M). Its thumbnail is the hot take itself: BEST and WORST over two covers.
6. **Time follows interest.** DIBZ gives filler games 10 to 46 seconds and the newest games 154 to 189 (S). Kingdom Hearts grows from 3.3 minutes an entry at the bottom to 7.5 near the top (M). Pulse gives equal slots.
7. **Commentary states its stance early and tests claims.** Verdict at 0:00, 0:31 and 2:15. Luke Stephens tests five sceptics' claims in 3 to 8.5 minutes each; The Act Man uses six numbered steps with cards (M). The flop took no stance and read out other people's posts (7.2 source words per 1,000, the highest in the set).
8. **Steel-man first, flag what is unknown.** Star Citizen spends 0:24 to 1:51 praising the game before attacking. Rumours are spoken as uncertain ("just estimates") (M).
9. **Music is cast by era or entry, and silence marks the seams.** 2.4 to 4.3 changes per 10 minutes in documentaries, about 8 in the ranking. Drop-outs at title and chapter cards in every watched documentary (S).
10. **Fast intro, calmer body.** Intros cut 1.5 to 2.5 times faster (S). Words in the first 30 seconds: 73 to 118 (M).
11. **Self-made pictures.** Driving Physics: about 21% custom 3D recreations. GMTK: about 25% schematics, plus a live experiment (he recompiled the game to show the trick). Luke Stephens does a live Street View comparison (S). Pulse shows what exists; they show what they made or tested.
12. **Evidence is visible and credited.** GMTK lists 23 sources and shows numbered citations. Geller names a source about 5 times per 1,000 words. Luke Stephens says 19.1 numbers per 1,000 words and puts each document on screen (M, S).
13. **Pace breathes.** Per-5-minute speed swings from 73 to 219 (NakeyJakey) and 96 to 195 (Noclip). The flop stayed between 172 and 223 for an hour (M). Human hits use short sentences: 27 to 70% are 8 words or fewer.
14. **Endings add something to the callback.** A verdict, a question to viewers, or a threat and a teaser. "It's in the name" at 0:17 returns as the last line at 25:59 (M). A callback alone did not save the flop.
15. **Packaging promises one thing to find out.** Thumbnail words: 0 to 4, adding to the title ("32mb", "CLONED", "IT'S FINALLY BACK"). The Long Road's thumbnail says only "EPISODE 1" over a road and the Rockstar logo: it sells an event. The copied GMTK template with a list topic got 0.53x (M).

**When a franchise history can work (I, from Army Men):** a neglected subject, a thesis (a cautionary tale stated at 1:10), exclusive material and one chapter per game. A neutral history of GTA, the most crowded subject in gaming, has none of these. That is the likeliest reason "Every Mainline GTA Game, Ranked" got 2 views: no verdict in the packaging, no person, no fight.

## 4. Rules a factory can follow

Each is a test, not a proven law; four more hits cannot prove cause.

### Writer

1. **One finish line per video.** The outline must name a single question or goal, the second it is planted (before 1:30) and the second it is paid (in the last 15%). Reject outlines without both.
2. **Open on a real voice.** The first 30 seconds contain a dated archive clip or a quoted primary source, before any narration of history.
3. **Voices per minute.** At least one non-narrator voice (archive clip, developer quote read over its source, Martin's recorded take) every 90 seconds in documentaries. Target 10 or more distinct named voices in a 30-minute documentary.
4. **Rankings: a verdict per entry.** Every entry ends with a one-line verdict in "I" form. At least 35% of sentences evaluative. State by 0:30 that viewers will disagree and name the most contested placement. Target "I" at 20 or more per 1,000 words.
5. **Rankings: unequal time.** Bottom third of entries under 60 seconds each; top three at least twice the median.
6. **Commentary: verdict by 2:15, then numbered claims.** Three to six claims, each opened with a spoken signpost, each closed with "so that one is true / half true / wrong". Steel-man the other side in the first two minutes. Mark every rumour as unconfirmed in the sentence that states it.
7. **Chapter hand-overs by contrast.** The last line of a chapter and the first of the next must carry opposite feelings (joy then threat, praise then cost).
8. **End on a verdict plus one of: a question to viewers, or the next threat.** For series, end at the lowest point.
9. **Vary the pace on purpose.** Each chapter gets one fast passage and one slow one; flag any 10 minutes where speed stays within 10% throughout. At least 30% of sentences 8 words or fewer.
10. **No franchise histories unless** the brief names a thesis, something exclusive and a finish line.

### Editor

1. **Two speeds.** Opening two minutes at 18 to 24 cuts a minute; body at 8 to 14. Quoted voices may hold up to 15 seconds.
2. **One made thing every three minutes.** A schematic, map, side-by-side or recreation that Pulse built, aiming for 20% of runtime in explainers.
3. **Era dressing.** Old footage and old news sit inside one consistent period frame; chapter cards share one design.
4. **Credit every voice on screen** with name and role for its first appearance.
5. **Show the document.** When a number or quote is spoken, its source is on screen in the same shot.
6. **Rankings: a rank card, the entry's own footage and no art held past 8 seconds.**
7. **Thumbnail: 0 to 4 words that are not in the title.** For rankings, show the verdict (best and worst). For a series, sell the event.
8. **Sponsor or channel plug only after the promise is complete**, never before 3:30.

### Mixer

1. **Music cast per chapter.** A new bed at each chapter, chosen for the era or mood of that chapter: 2.5 to 4 changes per 10 minutes in documentaries, one per entry in rankings.
2. **Silence at the seams.** A full drop-out of 0.5 to 1.5 seconds before the title card and each chapter card, and before the single most important line.
3. **No music under the opening archive clip.** Let room tone carry the first 20 to 30 seconds.
4. **Keep the source's own sound.** Archive clips keep their hum and hall; game clips keep engine, radio and crowd under the voice, ducked.
5. **Small tactile effects on graphics only:** one sound for cards, one for photographs, one for documents. Rankings get one lock-in sound per verdict.

### Community and cadence

1. **Pin a question that asks for the viewer's own ranking or answer.** GMTK's got 1,200 likes; DIBZ's got 69 replies.
2. **Series go out in the week of the event, two days apart, with a trailer three weeks before.** Expect later parts to hold a third to a half of part one.

## 5. What could not be measured

- **Loudness in LUFS and exact cut counts.** YouTube bot-checks downloads and I did not work round it, so no ffmpeg scene detection or ebur128. All cut rates, shot lengths, visual shares and music cues are vidIQ watch estimates (S).
- **Retention curves** for other channels. Every cause given for an underperformer is an inference.
- **Narrator-only word counts** in interview-led videos: captions do not name speakers, so pronoun rates for The Long Road, People Make Games and Driving Physics include guests.
- **Sentence lengths** for four videos whose captions have no punctuation.
- **Sound and visuals** for the 17 videos that were not watched.
- **Comments** are one page of 100 top threads per video, for nine videos.
- **Episodes 2 and 3 of The Long Road** were not transcribed; only their metadata was read.
- **LEMMiNO, Errant Signal, Mr Lore and TheCollapseCo** had no outlier in the window and were left out.
- **Pulse's own long videos** were not re-measured here; Pulse figures come from the 4 Nov study.
