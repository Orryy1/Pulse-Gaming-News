# Long-form gaming documentaries and essays: the yardstick (18 Nov 2026)

Thirteen videos measured against one fixed yardstick. Research only; nothing in the pipeline was changed.

**Labels.** **M** = Measured (computed by me from transcripts, metadata or comments on disk). **S** = Snippet (reported by vidIQ watch or read from a description; not verified frame by frame). **I** = Inferred (my reading).

**Where the files are.** The data lives in the base checkout: `C:\Users\MORR\gaming-studio\pulse-gaming\reports\research-2026-11-18\raw-long\`. That folder holds transcript*<id>.txt, watch*<id>.md (A, C, E, H), comments\_<id>.json (C, E, F) and docs_metadata.json (all 13: views, likes, comments, chapters, likes and comments per 1,000 views, title word count). This report sits at the worktree path because a session hook blocked writing into the base checkout.

**Caveats**

- H (Raycevick) and L (Gaming Historian) have auto-captions with no punctuation, so their sentence metrics are invalid. L's captions are comma-split word by word, so treat its numbers-per-1k figure with care.
- "Take cues" and "source cues" are phrase-count proxies ("I think", "the best", "perfect" and so on; "said", "according to", "study", "wrote" and so on) per 1,000 words. They count interviewees' words too.
- Chapter counts include Intro, Outro and Credits entries where creators list them.
- Wpm per 5 min includes music-only stretches, so low buckets usually mean footage or music, not slow talking.

## Cross-video craft patterns

### 1. The title sells a specific thing to find out, not a topic

- **Hits name a mechanism, a person or a paradox with a hard edge.**
  - C: "entire city into PS2 memory".
  - A: "Explained in 26 Minutes".
  - G: "took over my life".
  - H: "Perfection, Please Don't Buy It".
  - J: "used to look a lot better".
- **The two underperformers break this.**
  - B is a 5-word topic label ("Open Worlds in Video Games").
  - M is an inward-facing list about the channel's own jam.
- **Word count is not the lever.** Hits range from 3 to 14 words (M).

### 2. Thumbnails use 0-4 words, and the words add to the title rather than repeat it

- **Examples.** C "32mb", D "CLONED", A "IT'S FINALLY BACK", J "THE DEATH OF MINIATURES". F, G and H have no promise words at all and lean on one striking image (M/I).
- **Exceptions.** E and L repeat their series-format titles, but both are franchise-format channels.
- **The template is not the driver.** M copied C's template (a cut-out character over a giant number) and still got 0.53x against C's 3.5x. The promise behind the image drives it.

### 3. The question lands inside about 90 s, and is often half-answered at once

- **Timings (M).**
  - K 0:00.
  - J asks at 0:16 and answers at 0:31.
  - F 0:31.
  - C 0:45.
  - A asks at 1:29 and answers in short at 1:38.
  - I 1:56.
  - G 2:24, with the goal at 5:25.
  - E 2:29, after 2 min of proof.
- **The early answer is the hook.** The depth that follows is the reason to stay.
- **B asks three abstract questions at 1:09-1:25, then defines the term and lists types.** The sponsor runs at 4:11 (7% in), before any argument. Its hit sibling A asks one fan-war question and answers it within 9 s.

### 4. One long open loop with a concrete finish line carries the long videos

- **Examples (M).**
  - G: "10K" set at 5:25, paid at 1:18:35 (73 min).
  - F: assemble 4,000 panels in 3 days, set at 0:48, paid at about 46:30.
  - H: the title paradox, set at 0:00-0:51, paid at 31:42 and 35:52.
  - L: "the hype was real", set at 0:00, built through Part 2, closed at 45:44.
- **D pushes the loop outside the video** (a poll and a community post). It gets 7.07 comments per 1,000 views, the highest in the set.
- **Exceptions.** B and M plant no loop. E replaces the loop with a refrain ("something there, nothing there") that returns as the last line.

### 5. Structure is visible and named in searchable nouns or questions

- **Examples (M).**
  - C: 13 chapters, average 2.0 min ("The Pop-in Problem", "Memory Fragmentation").
  - A: 11 chapters, average 2.4 min.
  - I: 9 chapters, average 2.9 min.
  - K: chapters named as candidate questions ("Avowed?"), each re-asking the title.
  - F: diary days.
  - L: three named Parts.
- **B's chapters are vague and uneven.** "Vibes" runs 12.7 min.
- **M is the opposite failure.** 55 entries of 30 s each is a catalogue with no build.
- **Essays (D, E, G, H) skip a chapter list** but mark sections on screen with cards and stingers (S).

### 6. Endings call back to the opening line; strong ones add a verdict or a next step

- **Callbacks (M).**
  - A: "It's in the name" (0:17), then "live up to its name" (25:59).
  - C: magic trick (0:27, then 23:45).
  - L: hype (0:18, then 45:44).
  - E: something or nothing (about 11:00, then 45:47).
  - B: take a step, then pick a path.
- **The callback alone does not save a video.** B has one.
- **Most endings add a verdict (A, H, K) or a question for viewers (C, D, J).**
- **Teasers for the next video:** H, I, K.

### 7. Pace has no magic number, but variation does

- **Overall pace ranges from 118 wpm (L, the biggest hit) to 198 (B, the underperformer) (M).**
  - Explainers sit around 155-175.
  - Essays and documentaries sit around 120-150.
- **Hits let pace breathe.** Per-5-minute spread: G 73-219, F 123-192, I 96-195, L 108-143 with music bridges.
- **B is fast and flat for an hour** (172-223).
- **Words in the first 30 s do not predict much.** J 115 (14x) and H 0 (text cards) both work.

### 8. Music marks the seams; silence and diegetic sound make the punchlines

- **Music-only breathers sit on chapter boundaries (M).**
  - K: within 7 s of all four chapter starts.
  - L: before all three Parts.
- **Watched videos change music cue every 2.5-4 min (S).** H uses 34 tracks in 40 min (M, from the description).
- **Deliberate drop-outs (S).**
  - A at 0:54: the music stops for the TikTok clip.
  - C at 0:52: the disc-tray SFX.
  - H at 0:51: hard silence before the first word.
  - E at 10:57: music falls away for a reading.

### 9. Intros cut 1.5-2.5x faster than bodies, and explainers build their own visuals

- **Cuts per minute, intro against body (S).** A 24/14, C 19/10, H 31/14, E 6.4/4 (the slow essay still speeds up its intro).
- **Custom explanatory visuals are a large share in explainers (S).**
  - A: 21% 3D mannequin recreations.
  - C: 25% schematics.
  - E: 40% presenter on camera.
  - H: 68% gameplay, with comparison triptychs.

### 10. A personal stake buys loyalty and comments; an impersonal voice rides big topics

- **High "I" rates go with personal stakes and higher comment rates.**
  - G: 60.7 I per 1,000 words, 3.01 comments per 1,000 views.
  - D: 39.0 and 7.07.
  - F: 34.2 and 3.01.
- **Near-zero "I" videos have the biggest raw reach and the lowest like rates.**
  - L: 2.0 I, 15.9 likes per 1,000 views.
  - J: 1.2 and 14.0.
  - A: 1.7 and 23.1.
- **Take density.** H (6.3/1k) and G (4.5/1k) are the most opinionated; both have the highest like rates among the essays.

### 11. Authority is shown, not just asserted

- **Sources are named on air or cited on screen (M/S).**
  - E: source cues 5.0/1k, with study titles on screen.
  - L: 3.7/1k (named developers and press).
  - C: bracketed [n] citations on screen, 23 sources in the description.
  - D: 28 sources in the description.
- **J (0.0/1k) is the counter-example.** It is broad film nostalgia carried by unnamed expert soundbites.

### 12. Sponsor placement protects the opening

- **Where each video puts its sponsor or ask (M).**
  - G: sponsor at 13:38, after the goal is set.
  - F: Nebula at about 41:00.
  - C: no sponsor; Patreon roll at the end.
  - E: Patreon only in the pinned comment.
  - B: sponsor at 4:11, inside the first 7%, before the argument begins.
- **Comments are also used as a lever.** Pinned creator comments that ask for the next topic (C, 1,200 likes) or push a companion piece (F) turn the comment section into a programming tool.

### What the underperformers did differently

**B against A** (same channel, same narrator):

- A generic topic title and a 2-word abstract thumbnail, against a timely title with a duration and a before/after thumbnail.
- 60 min, against 27.
- Three abstract questions, then a definition and a taxonomy, against one community argument answered in 9 s.
- Sponsor at 4:11.
- Flat 198 wpm.
- Generic second-person "you" at 35.8/1k, against 13.
- Its engagement among actual viewers was high (49.9 likes per 1,000 views), so packaging and the first minutes are the likely failure points (I).

**M against C and D** (same presenter): same voice, pace, humour and even thumbnail template. The difference is no question, no stakes and an inward topic, cut into 55 thirty-second entries.
