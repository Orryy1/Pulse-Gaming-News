# What human Shorts creators do that automated channels do not

Research only, written Wednesday 18 November 2026 for Pulse Gaming. No factory code or settings were changed. Nothing was posted, bought or downloaded. This builds on "Niche deep research November 2026" (strategy) and "Inspiration playlist forensics" (four long videos). The new angle here is craft inside the Short itself, measured on one yardstick.

## Summary in ten lines

1. I measured 27 breakout gaming Shorts and 7 flops from the same channels on one yardstick (34 Shorts, 24 channels, 197 to 7.1 million views).
2. The hits and the flops are edited the same way. Pace, cuts, captions, music and sound effects are close to identical (212 against 223 words a minute, 3.3 against 2.8 cuts per 10 seconds). Polish is not what separates them.
3. What separates them is the gap. 27 of 27 hits open a question the viewer wants answered and hold the answer back. 2 of 7 flops do. The main payoff lands at 87% of the runtime in the middle hit. Two flops give the answer in the first three seconds.
4. No hit opens with a greeting, a sentence starting "So" or a plain list announcement. 3 of 7 flops do.
5. News hits are takes, not announcements. 8 of 9 state an opinion, with a middle count of 4 each. The same creators' plain news Shorts are their flops (lukogta: a how-to at 66.7 times its usual views, a release-date news Short at 0.10 times).
6. Comments follow opinions. Across the 27 hits the number of stated takes tracks comments per 1,000 views very closely (rank correlation 0.81). Sound effects and fast cutting do not (minus 0.44 and minus 0.25).
7. Being faceless is not the handicap: 18 of 27 hits show no person. An AI voice is not the handicap either: 8 of 27 hits use one, including L-LAB, which says so in every description.
8. The handicap of automated channels is silence around the video. AI-voiced hits get 0.16 comments per 1,000 views against 1.13 for human-voiced hits. None of the eight AI-voiced hits says "I" once.
9. Hits run long and full: the middle hit is 62 seconds, 15 of 27 pass one minute and 19 of 27 never show the same shot twice.
10. For Pulse this means: write every Short as one held-back answer with one stated take in a consistent voice, turn news into "why" or "what you can do", show a new picture every 3 seconds and let the game speak now and then.

## How to read the numbers

Every number carries one of these labels.

- **Measured**: public YouTube figures pulled through vidIQ or yt-dlp listings on 18 Nov 2026 (views, likes, comments, subscribers, length, vidIQ outlier score), or counted by script from them (comments per 1,000 views, views against the channel's own median).
- **Measured (watcher)**: counted by script from the walkthrough that vidIQ's AI watcher wrote for each Short (spoken words, sentence breaks, cut times, captions, overlays, music, sound effects, pauses). I checked its transcript against YouTube's own captions on one Short and the words matched exactly. Its cut and sound-effect counts are a careful viewer's count, not a frame-accurate one. Treat them as approximate.
- **Inferred**: my own coding of what the watcher described (hook type, where the gap opens, turn, payoff, ending, number of stated opinions and jokes, whether a Short is tied to a current release). One coder, one pass.
- **Snippet**: seen in a vidIQ result list only, not opened.

Public view counts include swipes since August 2026, so they cannot be compared one for one with Pulse's engaged views. All comparisons here are between public figures.

"Outlier" is given two ways: vidIQ's outlier score (views against the channel's typical video, Measured) and, for the ten channels whose Shorts lists I pulled, views against the median of that channel's last 80 Shorts (Measured). "n/m" means not measured.

## The sample

- **Hits (27)**: 12 story, fact or "why" Shorts, 9 news takes and 6 rankings. Each is a breakout against its own channel (vidIQ outlier 2.4 to 1,130, middle value 9.7).
- **Flops (7)**: the weakest mature Shorts from six of the same channels (0.03 to 0.19 of the channel's median) plus one Mithrie news Short as the templated counter-example. The Mithrie Short is from 2021, so read it as an example of the form, not of this month.
- Channels range from 135 subscribers (Hitbox Stories) to 781,000 (Luke Stephens). 14 of the 27 hits come from channels under 20,000 subscribers.

## The patterns that separate hits from flops and from automated channels

Each pattern gives its evidence count. Hits n = 27, flops n = 7 unless stated. Seven flops is a small group: read the flop figures as direction, not proof.

### 1. The gap is the whole game, not the polish

- Open question planted: 27 of 27 hits, 2 of 7 flops (Inferred).
- Payoff lands after 60% of the runtime: 22 of 27 hits (middle value 87%), 3 of 7 flops (Inferred).
- Two flops answer their own hook at once. Hitbox Stories' elevator Short states the answer in its title and again in its second sentence at 2 seconds (0.19 of channel median). Its hit asks "why" in the title and pays at 85% (5.0 times median). lukogta's news flop gives the date in sentence one (0.10) while its hit takes 37 seconds to reach the take-off (66.7).
- Editing does not explain the difference. Hits against flops, middle values: 212 against 223 words a minute, 3.3 against 2.8 cuts per 10 seconds, 0.36 against 0.44 sound effects per 10 seconds, same caption style, music in 26 of 27 against 6 of 7 (Measured, watcher).

This is the most useful finding for an automated channel: a better edit of the same script would not have saved the flops.

### 2. Title and first line say one idea, and the first line is short

- Title and first spoken line state the same idea: 24 of 27 hits, 12 of 12 story hits (Inferred). In at least four hits the first line is the title almost word for word.
- Hook length: middle value 11 words, 18 of 27 at 12 words or fewer (Measured, watcher).
- Gap open by 3 seconds in 15 of 27, by 5 seconds in 20, by 7 seconds in 25 (Measured, watcher).
- Extra text on the first frame beyond the caption: none in 17 of 27. When there is some it is the evidence itself (the post, the comment being answered, a stopwatch) or the title as a banner, never a second idea (Measured, watcher).
- The one flop where title, on-screen header and first line were three different ideas is DanJamanta's "$400" Short (0.16 of median). The price in the title is never spoken.

### 3. Five hook shapes cover most hits, and three shapes never appear

Hook types in the 27 hits (Inferred): surprising claim 7, question 5, contradiction 4, list premise 4, hot take 2, and one each of how-to with a date, cold-open reaction, reading out a quote, replying to a comment and a relatable-irony list.

- Contradiction is the strongest shape per use: "they said X, the truth is Y", "aren't doing X, they're doing Y", "some are earned, these are not". 4 hits, including the 4.9 million and 3.0 million view Shorts.
- Shapes with zero hits: a greeting, a sentence starting "So" and a plain "here are some games" list. Those three open 3 of the 7 flops.
- Only 6 of 27 hits open with a literal question, so "always ask a question" is not the rule. The rule is an unresolved claim.

### 4. News works as a take or an explanation, not as an announcement

- 8 of 9 news-take hits contain a stated opinion (middle value 4 per Short), 7 of 9 say "I" and 8 of 9 say "you" (Measured, watcher and Inferred). The ninth reads out other people's posts and lets a community note deliver the verdict.
- 8 of 9 open on something other than the announcement: a consequence ("picked the worst time"), an anecdote (a named developer played two hours and never reached a mission), a quote, a hostile comment or a reaction. The one that opens on a bare "just confirmed" is the fabricated Short in pattern 15.
- Same-channel contrast (Measured): Joeshi 2's "why" Short at 31.8 times median against its "games coming in November" list at 0.11. lukogta's how-to at 66.7 against "PC release date confirmed" at 0.10. Luke Stephens' reaction and review Shorts at 6 to 11 times against his retro-fact and sponsor Shorts at 0.06 to 0.12.
- Title study (563 titles, ten channels, Measured): titles with a news word (confirmed, update, release date, announced, details, coming) are high variance: 31.1% reach 3 times the channel median against 19.5% for other titles, but 22.2% fall below a third of it against 13.1% (n = 45). News is a lottery ticket. A take on news is the safer form of the same ticket.
- 17 of 27 hits are tied to the release of the month (Ocarina of Time remake, GTA VI, Wolverine) but are built as "why", "how to", "ranked" or "my take" (Inferred). Human-voiced hits are event-tied in 12 of 14 cases, AI-voiced hits in 1 of 8.

### 5. Comments follow stated opinions

- Rank correlation with comments per 1,000 views across the 27 hits (Measured against Inferred counts): number of stated takes 0.81, "I" per 100 words 0.63, "we" per 100 words 0.54, length 0.48, jokes 0.20, "you" per 100 words minus 0.09, cuts per 10 seconds minus 0.25, sound effects per 10 seconds minus 0.44.
- Comments per 1,000 views, middle values (Measured): news takes 1.86, rankings 1.71, story and fact Shorts 0.26.
- Likes do not follow the same rule (takes against likes %: 0.10). Opinions buy talk, not approval.
- Ending on a question is not what does it. Only 5 of 27 hits end on a question or a call to action, and their comment rates run from 0.48 to 5.58.
- What the top comments are (25 top threads read on each of 7 hits, Measured): the viewer's own answer or a correction. hChatNation's top comment (11,900 likes) offers a different explanation from the video's. Joeshi 2's second most liked comment (862 likes) rejects the script's "we". lukogta's are viewers' own plans for the drive. TDAWG's are votes for a favourite. A Short that leaves room to disagree or to add gets the comments.

### 6. The voice is a person with a view, even when no person is shown

- At least one stated take: 22 of 27 hits. At least one joke: 19 of 27 (Inferred).
- "I" appears in 13 of 27 hits and 9 of 14 human-voiced hits. "You" appears in 20 of 27 (Measured, watcher).
- Human scripts sound spoken: 37% of their sentences start with But, So, And, Then or Now, against 17% in AI-voiced hits. A "but" pivot sentence appears in 17 of 27 hits and 2 of 7 flops (Measured, watcher).
- Criticism is specific and aimed at a decision, a company or a design, and it usually arrives with a joke (a villain who looks like he is hiding a bald spot, a game that seems to run on a toaster). The one hit that criticises a person directly (KidShadoe's reply to a negative commenter) does it as a defence of fans and has the highest comment rate in the set, 7.15 per 1,000.
- Rankings carry the most takes: middle value 7.5 per Short, and every entry gets a verdict in a sentence.

### 7. A new picture every 3 seconds, and almost never the same one twice

- All hits: 3.3 cuts per 10 seconds, one picture per 2.8 seconds (middle values). Story hits: 2.7 seconds per shot. First cut at 2.8 seconds, within 3 seconds in 18 of 27 (Measured, watcher).
- Repeated shots: none in 19 of 27 hits.
- A face changes the sums. Hits with the creator on screen cut far less (1.3 per 10 seconds) because the face is the picture. A faceless channel cannot borrow that pace: its benchmark is the story hit rate.
- 26 of 27 hits open on moving footage. The one still (a producer's photo) has an animated overlay within 2 seconds.
- The picture is the thing being said. In the 4.9 million view hit the footage switches between the old and the new game eight times as the script compares them.

### 8. Captions are 1 to 3 words, and overlays are receipts

- Captions show 1 to 3 words at a time in 25 of 27 hits and are word-by-word in 23 (Measured, watcher).
- At least one overlay in 24 of 27, at a middle rate of 0.9 per 10 seconds. Almost all are evidence: the article headline, the post, the source's name and role, a box cover, a score, a crossed-out date, a stopwatch counting the two hours in the story, a credit for someone's clip.
- Arrows and circles are rare: 3 hits use one, each once, on the exact thing being named.

### 9. Sound is a quiet bed, and the game gets to speak

- Music under the voice in 26 of 27. It changes or drops in only 2 (Measured, watcher).
- Sound effects are sparse: middle value 0.36 per 10 seconds, none at all in 10 of 27. Where they appear they mark an overlay arriving or are the game's own sounds (a cash chime, a pickup, a chicken).
- Game audio is audible under the voice in 19 of 27.
- Three story hits stop narrating and let the game or a clip talk for 10 to 25 seconds (L-LAB twice, Joeshi 2 once). L-LAB's 1.35 million view hit ends on a character's line, not the narrator's.
- A deliberate pause appears in 8 of 27 (a 1.5 second silence as the whole verdict on one game, a black frame for a game that crashes).
- I could not measure loudness, ducking depth or exact effect counts. See "Not measured".

### 10. Hits end on the punchline and stop

- Ending type (Inferred): punchline 18, call to action 2, question plus call to action 2, and one each of loop, abrupt, summary, cliff and question.
- 22 of 27 hits end with no ask at all. 3 of 7 flops end on a call to action.
- A true loop back to the first line appears once (Hitbox Stories, cut mid-sentence on "because"). Looping is not what the human hits rely on.
- Of the two hits that ask for a follow, one attaches a reason and a deadline ("the rest of it before November 19th").

### 11. Hits are long and full

- Length (Measured): middle value 62 seconds. 2 hits at 30 seconds or under, 5 at 31 to 45, 5 at 46 to 60, 10 at 61 to 75, 5 over 75.
- Words: middle value 195, at 212 words a minute (range 168 to 301). Rankings are the densest: 274 words in 76 seconds.
- Average sentence is 16.3 words, longer than our written rule, because spoken sentences chain with "and" and "but". The AI-voiced hits are shorter (14.7), and the only one written in fragments is Hitbox Stories (7.1).

### 12. Series are shapes, not labels

- Named series are rare in titles: 0 of 27 hit titles carry a part number or a series name (Measured).
- What recurs is the sentence shape of the title. L-LAB: "What happens if...". Hitbox Stories: "Why can't you...". Epix Gaming: "Ranking EVERY...". hChatNation: one franchise and one remake across dozens of Shorts. NEUTORR: "Why does X have...?" (Snippet, 1.6 and 2.1 million views).
- At least 6 of the 12 story hits come from channels that cover one franchise or one publisher (hChatNation, Joeshi 2 twice, L-LAB twice, lukogta). I did not check the rest (Inferred).

### 13. Title mechanics barely matter

From 563 titles across ten channels, views against each channel's own median (Measured):

- Question mark: 0.94 against 1.00 without. Starts with a question word: 1.07 against 0.96.
- An all-caps emphasis word: 0.93 against 1.01, with more flops (19.2% against 12.3%).
- A number: 0.89 against 1.01. An ellipsis: 1.17 against 0.99 (n = 47).
- "I" or "My" in the title: 0.77 against 1.02, and fewer breakouts (13.7% against 21.1%). The take belongs in the voice, not in a diary-style title.
- Length: titles of 1 to 5 words reach 3 times median 28.3% of the time, 9 to 11 words 13.9%.
- Naming GTA 6: 1.12 against 0.95, breakouts 25.2% against 19.2%.

So there is no magic title pattern to automate. Short titles that name the game and state the premise do slightly better. That fits Pulse's own result that "GTA 6 Why Its..." titles failed: the wording was neither the problem nor the cure. The premise behind it was.

### 14. Automated channels can reach, but they do not convert or converse

- AI-voiced hits against human-voiced hits, middle values (Measured): comments per 1,000 views 0.16 against 1.13. Stated takes 1.0 against 2.5. "I" used: 0 of 8 against 9 of 14.
- Channel totals (Measured): Hitbox Stories, fully animated with an AI voice, has 135 subscribers from 544,353 views, 0.25 per 1,000. Joeshi 2 (human voice, takes) has 3,590 from 5.78 million, 0.62. DanJamanta (human voice, replies to commenters) has 1,710 from 2.70 million, 0.63.
- Creator replies in the top 25 threads (Measured): DanJamanta replied in 9 and has his own comment at the top linking the long video. hChatNation and KidShadoe replied once each. The other four did not reply in the sample.
- Story hits travel furthest beyond the subscriber base: 181.7 views per subscriber against 24.6 for news takes and 8.2 for rankings (Measured, middle values). Story Shorts find strangers. Takes and rankings feed the people already there.

### 15. A warning: one "hit" is a fabrication

Brutan's 765,000 view Short (254.7 times its median) says a publisher "has just confirmed" an update, then hedges every later line with "reportedly", "insiders claim" and "allegedly". It uses an AI voice. The form works on the feed, and it is exactly what Pulse's verified-sources rule forbids. It is in the tables as a hit because it is one, not as a model.

## Rules a writer, an editor and a sound mixer could follow

Each rule is written so a script or a check can enforce it. The thresholds come from the hit ranges above.

### Script writer

1. **One held-back answer.** State the question or contradiction in sentence one. The sentence that answers it must not start before 60% of the word count. Reject any script whose first two sentences contain the answer, and any title that states the answer when the script's job is to reveal it.
2. **Hook of 12 words or fewer** that is complete by 3 seconds at the narration speed. Allowed shapes: surprising claim, contradiction ("X, but Y"), a real question, a list premise with a limit ("every... in 60 seconds"), a stated hot take. Banned first words: So, Today, Hey, Welcome, Here are, Hello. Banned first sentences: a date, a release announcement, a greeting.
3. **Title and first line are one idea.** Check by word overlap or by a judge: a viewer who reads the title and hears line one must not meet two topics. Titles of 8 words or fewer, naming the game.
4. **A news story must be re-angled before it is written.** Pick one: why it happened, what it changes for the player, what the player can do about it before a date, or the narrator's verdict. If none fits, it is not a Short.
5. **At least one stated take in the narrator's own voice** ("I think", "I'll take the wait"), placed in the last third. News takes and rankings need three or more, one per item for a ranking. Aim criticism at a decision, never at players, and pair it with a light joke.
6. **One "but" pivot** in the middle third.
7. **Spoken joins.** About a third of sentences should start with But, So, And, Then or Now. Contractions throughout. Two or three sentences of six words or fewer for punch.
8. **Leave a door open.** Say one thing a fan could fairly disagree with or add to, and do not settle it. Do not end on "let me know in the comments". End on the punchline or the take and stop.
9. **Length follows content.** 150 to 250 words for a story or a take, up to 380 for a ranking. Do not cut a full script down to 30 seconds.
10. **Never state a rumour as confirmed.** The existing verified-sources rule stays.

### Editor

1. **Opening frame moves** and shows the subject of the first line. No title card, no black frame, no logo.
2. **First cut by 3 seconds.** After that one new picture every 2.5 to 3.5 seconds on average, never longer than 6 seconds on one shot unless the game is speaking.
3. **No shot twice** inside one Short. Fail the render if any clip is reused.
4. **The picture is the noun.** Each sentence shows the thing it names. A comparison shows both sides, cut against each other.
5. **Captions 1 to 3 words**, word by word, centre or lower centre, one colour with an outline, with a highlight on the key word at most.
6. **Overlays are receipts only**: source headline, post, name and role, box cover, score, date, counter. About one per 10 seconds. An arrow or circle only on the single thing being named, at most once.
7. **Hold the last frame on the payoff** and stop. No end card, and no subscribe animation unless the line gives a reason and a date.
8. **Rankings**: the item's cover or name card pops in on the number, the footage is that item and the verdict lands before the next number.

### Sound mixer

1. **One music bed**, low, start to finish. No track changes inside a Short.
2. **Sound effects only where something arrives on screen** or as the game's own sound. Budget: 0 to 4 per story Short, and none is fine. No effect on ordinary cuts.
3. **Keep game audio audible** under the voice wherever the footage has it.
4. **Once per story Short where the footage allows, let the game speak**: duck the music, stop the narrator and play 3 to 10 seconds of in-game dialogue or the sound being discussed, with captions.
5. **One silence beat** of 0.7 to 1.5 seconds is allowed before a punchline. No other gaps over 0.4 seconds.
6. **Narration at 190 to 230 words a minute** after tightening gaps.

### After upload (the part the factory does not do yet)

1. Put a comment at the top within the hour that either links the long video or states the narrator's take as a claim to argue with.
2. Reply to the first ten real comments in the narrator's voice, agreeing or disagreeing with a reason.
3. Track comments per 1,000 views per Short beside views. The range in this sample's human-voiced hits is 0.2 to 7.2, with takes and rankings at about 1.8.

## Not measured, and where to be careful

- **Retention, swipe-away and returning viewers** are private. Everything here is inferred from public counts.
- **Frame-accurate cuts, loudness and music ducking.** YouTube blocked yt-dlp downloads and captions with its bot check, so no file was analysed. Cuts, effects and pauses are the AI watcher's counts.
- **Human or AI voice.** The watcher's call is unreliable. Only L-LAB's is confirmed by disclosure. The AI-voiced group (8 hits) is therefore partly a judgement.
- **Pinned comments and full reply behaviour.** The comment data does not flag pins, and I read the top 25 threads on 7 of the 27 hits, not all.
- **Thumbnails.** Not looked at. First-frame text is the watcher's reading.
- **Subscribers gained per Short.** Only channel totals are public.
- **Channel-median outlier ratio** exists for ten channels. The other hits rely on vidIQ's score.
- **Sample bias.** English only, chosen from outlier lists filtered to 3% likes or more and from named channels. Seven flops, one of them old. Hook types, takes and jokes were coded by one reader.
- **Correlation, not cause.** The 0.81 between takes and comments is across 27 Shorts and partly reflects format: news takes and rankings carry both more opinions and more arguable subjects.

vidIQ credits used by this study: 405 of the 600 allowed (34 Short watches, 7 comment pulls, 3 outlier searches, 2 lookups and 1 transcript check). Other sessions were drawing on the same vidIQ account at the same time, so the account balance fell by more than that. No money was spent.
