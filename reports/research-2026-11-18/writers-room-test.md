# Writers' room test, 19 Nov 2026

The same 24 published stories as the earlier blind tests, taken through a fuller process on the PC with Opus helpers. No API spend, and nothing changed on the Mac mini.

## The process tested

1. **Fact pack.** A researcher finds the real reason, up to three word-for-word quotes, the hard numbers, the least-known true detail, what players are arguing about and one earlier event that gives the story meaning. Anything it cannot confirm goes on an "unverified" list.
2. **Writers.** Four angles per story, then three drafts on the best two angles.
3. **Two critics.** A tough editor and an audience stand-in mark each draft blind and name its one biggest fault.
4. **Edit.** The winner is fixed against those faults.
5. **Voice pass.** A rewrite by ear: a form chosen for the story, no padding, no shape shared across the set.
6. **Pick.** The better of the edited and voiced versions is kept. Thin stories are dropped.

## Scores

Two blind judges used the same brief as the earlier rounds, with every version mixed into one sitting. These sittings marked about 0.8 lower than the 18 Nov sitting on the same scripts, so compare within this table, not with the 6.9 reported on 18 Nov.

| Version | Overall, out of 10 |
|---|---|
| Published originals (4 of them) | 3.4 |
| Top human creators' breakout Shorts (6) | 4.7 |
| Editor's loop rewrites from 18 Nov (8) | 5.9 |
| Writers' room after the edit (24) | 6.9 |
| After the voice pass (24) | 7.0 |
| Best version per story (24) | 7.3 |
| Best version, the five dropped stories left out (19) | 7.4 |
| Best version, the weakest quarter left out (18) | 7.5 |

- Of the 19 kept stories, 12 scored 7.5 or more, 5 scored 8 or more and 1 was under 7. One script got a 9 from one judge. None got 9 from both.
- The voice pass lifted "sounds human" from 5.9 to 7.1, level with the human creators (7.2), and clarity to 7.7. It cost some payoff: several scripts ended up with the answer in the first line, and 6 of 24 got worse.
- The audience stand-in expected viewers to finish 23 of the 24 final scripts, against 2 of 8 earlier rewrites, 1 of 4 originals and 0 of 6 human Shorts. Treat the human figure with care: human hits win on footage, topic and delivery, which a script reader cannot see.

## What moved the score, in order

1. **Better material (the fact pack).** Worth about one full point on its own. The best angles came from the least-known true detail and the earlier event. The endings came from real quotes and numbers.
2. **Fewer names and figures.** At most two spoken names besides the game and its maker, and two numbers. Sources go on screen.
3. **The answer late, by cutting.** Never by adding words before it.
4. **Plain endings.** No slogans. Often just the reveal, or one dry line.
5. **Variety.** A different form for each story. One fixed shape is spotted at once when scripts are read side by side.
6. **Dropping thin stories.** About a fifth of these stories could not carry a Short however they were written.

## What it could not fix

- Stories whose honest answer is small. They need a different story in the slot.
- The pull between a held-back answer and a natural voice. Keeping both versions and choosing per story works better than one rule.
- Marks above 8. The judges gave the best human creator Short a 7.

- A late answer showed no gain on Pulse's own Shorts, because the late-answer Shorts so far were the weak "Why" ones. That question stays with the record-only test on the Mac mini.

## Six before and after

### GTA 6 Online's Lobby Size, Revealed in Court

**Published (5.5 out of 10):** Does your GTA 6 preload include GTA Online? Dataminers digging through the preload reportedly found lines like 'launch Multiplayer Activity' and 'join Session.' But Rockstar has called GTA 6 a single-player game, and GameSpot expects Online to arrive later. Which fits the next find. Story is its own download, and Twitch's CEO has said Online drops in twenty twenty-seven. No, and we wouldn't plan your launch weekend around it. Those strings point to later, and the base game ships without Online on November 19. Until then, what's sitting in your preload is a single-player game.

**Writers' room (8.0 out of 10, 64 words):** Rockstar let slip GTA 6's online lobby size in court. It still hasn't announced online at all. Last year it fired staff over posts in a chat server, and they fought it. Defending that, Rockstar's filing called the online format "one of the most closely guarded secrets of the company." Then it named the format. Thirty-two players. GTA Online already holds 30. Some secret.

### Blizzard told WoW fans they were wrong

**Published (5.0 out of 10):** World of Warcraft Classic exists because expansions kept deleting what you loved. In two thousand eight, Blizzard's Drysc rejected legacy servers. His reason: "We'd effectively be developing and supporting two different games." Except every expansion cut something. The Burning Crusade erased Kazzak and his loot. Wrath of the Lich King removed old Naxxramas and Onyxia. Blizzard announced Classic at BlizzCon twenty seventeen, and it launched in twenty nineteen. Set that two thousand eight refusal against twenty nineteen, when Classic won PC Game of the Year at the Golden Joysticks. If your favorite raid vanished, you got it back. Tomorrow brings another Why, so why did Blizzard wait 11 years?

**Writers' room (8.0 out of 10, 87 words):** Blizzard told World of Warcraft fans they didn't want the original game. Fans had asked for the old version back, and Blizzard's J. Allen Brack answered with a line people still quote: "You think you do, but you don't." So fans built it anyway. An unofficial server called Nostalrius ran the original game for 150,000 active players, until Blizzard shut it down. Then Blizzard met that team, and they shared their code. A year and a half later, Brack walked on stage and announced WoW Classic. Himself.

### NBA 2K27 missed, and Take-Two's boss shrugged

**Published (2.0 out of 10):** Grand Theft Auto six's preorder success shouldn't rush you. Polygon reports NBA 2K 27 missed sales expectations after the series' second-biggest launch. And Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick says GTA 6 preorders are running “far in excess” of expectations. But both games feed Take-Two. Polygon suggests GTA 6 may be pulling attention away from NBA 2K 27. NBA 2K 27 shows why we'd separate GTA six's sales forecasts from quality. You can wait for reviews. The company can hit its target without setting yours.

**Writers' room (7.5 out of 10, 70 words):** NBA 2K27 missed its sales target, and Take-Two's boss blamed GTA 6. His own company's game. 2K still sold about 4 million copies, just not as many as Take-Two forecast. But with GTA 6 days from launch, he said it had influenced shoppers. And he sounded pretty relaxed about it. Here's how he put it: "If your lunch is going to be eaten, I'd rather be the one eating it."

### GTA 5's Character Swap Hid a Secret

**Published (4.0 out of 10):** Did switching between Grand Theft Auto five's characters limit your helicopter flying? Technical designer Matt Freedman told IGN making the camera switch between characters took about two years. That's for roughly three minutes of gameplay. You could even fly Trevor's helicopter upside down. The camera still had to switch smoothly between him, Franklin and Michael. Rockstar still insisted you see the skyline. That meant less detail in distant buildings while the world loaded around you. Some scenery could pop in, but you kept helicopter control. That's a smart compromise. I'd take it. GTA five's camera adapted to your flying.

**Writers' room (8.0 out of 10, 95 words):** Two years of work, for maybe three minutes of GTA 5. That's how designer Matt Freedman describes the character swaps in the middle of missions. Rockstar's bosses wanted the city skyline on screen the whole time. No cutting away. But the old consoles couldn't hold the city in memory twice. So he cheated. During the swap, far-off traffic shrank to dots of light and distant buildings went soft and blurry. That blur was the game loading the new area. He says you can still catch it if you look far off through a sniper scope.

### The Doom number behind Xbox's id cuts

**Published (5.0 out of 10):** id Software lost half its studio, and Wolfenstein could gain. Co-director Marty Stratton says id is working more closely with MachineGames than ever. That's not the odd part. Stratton kept mentioning technology, and the cuts hit id's Frankfurt engine team. IGN's Ryan McCaffrey speculates they could co-develop the rumored Wolfenstein 3. Nothing's confirmed. Fair enough, losing half a studio is nobody's good news. Still, we think pairing the teams behind Doom and Indiana Jones is the smartest move left. If Wolfenstein 3 is real, you could get it sooner. Which shooter do you want id and MachineGames making together?

**Writers' room (8.0 out of 10, 69 words):** One Doom number probably explains why Xbox gutted id Software. Doom: The Dark Ages pulled in over 3 million players. Xbox's memo said it was focusing on its strongest franchises, and Doom's about as strong as Xbox gets. But most of those players came through Game Pass. Sales were reportedly nearer 1 million. Three million players sounds like a hit right up until someone asks how many bought it.

### Is Warhammer Survivors just a cheap copy?

**Published (4.0 out of 10):** Is Warhammer Survivors worth your seven dollars nineteen? PC Gamer describes an invulnerable tank follower, which we'd happily recruit. It attacks from its own position. That seven dollars nineteen Steam price gets you Warhammer forty thousand and Warhammer Age of Sigmar, each with separate progression. Except Xbox Wire confirms it's also on Game Pass, so check your subscription first. We'd call it good value. Each setting has its own heroes, maps and upgrades. Your first tactical decision happens before checkout.

**Writers' room (8.0 out of 10, 57 words):** Warhammer Survivors sounds like a Vampire Survivors rip-off in Warhammer armor. It costs eight bucks and borrows half the name. You mow down endless hordes. One stage is a miniature painting desk. Every sign points to a quick licensed cash-in, apart from one name in the credits. Poncle. The studio that made Vampire Survivors helped build it.
