## Line 1 - Two speakers, a whole space (Frame 1)

    Headphones have only two speakers, yet a game can place footsteps behind you and above. The trick begins before either channel reaches your ears.

## Line 2 - Position the object (Frame 2)

    A spatial audio engine treats each sound as an object with a position, then calculates how that direction should arrive at your left and right ears.

## Line 3 - Shape the cues (Frame 3)

    Tiny timing, level and frequency differences come from the head and outer ear. Together, those direction-dependent cues form an H-R-T-F.

## Line 4 - Follow the listener (Frame 4)

    Turn the camera and the filters change. Each channel's waveform shifts, while your brain interprets the pattern as a sound moving through space.

## Line 5 - Ears are personal (Frame 5)

    Ears differ, so one generic profile will not feel equally precise for everyone. Personalised profiles can improve the match where a system supports them.

## Line 6 - The last renderer (Frame 6)

    There are still only two channels at the end. The three-dimensional stage happens in the filtering - and your brain finishes the render.
