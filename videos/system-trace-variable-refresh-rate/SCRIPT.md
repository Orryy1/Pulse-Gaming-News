## Line 1 - VRR changes timing (Frame 1)

    Variable refresh rate does not make the GPU faster. It changes when a compatible monitor begins its next refresh.

## Line 2 - Fixed display, variable delivery (Frame 2)

    A fixed display keeps ticking at one rate, even while game frames finish early, late or somewhere between.

## Line 3 - Refresh when ready (Frame 3)

    With VRR, the monitor can refresh when the next frame is ready, reducing repeated frames, stutter and tearing inside its range.

## Line 4 - The timing window (Frame 4)

    That range has limits. A display might vary between sixty-four and one hundred and twenty hertz, not from zero to infinity.

## Line 5 - Outside the range (Frame 5)

    Fall below the lower bound and frames may be repeated. Exceed the top and familiar fixed-refresh constraints return.

## Line 6 - Timing flexibility (Frame 6)

    VRR is therefore timing flexibility, not free performance. The GPU, display, connection and presentation path must all support it.
